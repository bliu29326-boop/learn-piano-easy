# 0014: Every song of ours ends on an Original that sounds like the piece

## Decision

- Every song of ours whose tune is not our own ends on an Original step.
- Where the catalog holds the piece's own piano score, that score stays the Original.
- Every other Original is our own arrangement of the whole piece, set for two hands, under the MIT
  License.
- Our Original follows the source: its form, key, meter, tempo, harmony and its own accompaniment.
- It adds no bar of our own. A long piece is cut to its best-known sections, kept in their order.
- It keeps none of the four versions' limits. It keeps every check a hand sets: a finger on every note
  and a practical span.
- The meter rates it as the lowest version whose limits it fits, and as Advanced past Hard.
- The Original is the last step of its card, whatever its rating.
- Our Original carries a guide, the same as the four versions.
- After the Originals, a second pass makes the four versions closer to their source. It replaces every
  bar of our own with the source's next real phrase. Medium and Hard take the source's own accompaniment
  where the limits allow it.
- A reworked version is written to a new file, since a learner's hand-set fingers are keyed by note
  index.

## Why

The user found that our songs lose the original's sound. Each version is built from a short tune and
shaped by its level's rules. Its accompaniment comes from a list per level, such as an Alberti bass,
and not from the piece. Many songs add bars of our own after the tune.

Only four songs had an Original step, because only four originals are in the catalog. The other
seventy-three songs ended at Hard, so a learner never met the piece as it sounds.

An Original of our own fills that step without a new place in the app. The step, its colour and the
offer after Hard already exist.

The Originals come first because they add files and change none. The pass over the four versions
changes files that hold a learner's work, so it follows once the Originals have shown the way.

## Consequences

- A song of ours is five MIDI files and one guide, except the three tunes of our own.
- The meter gains a rating for an Original and a check that every song has one.
- The Original's tagline gets a second text for our arrangement, since "the composer's own notes" is
  false for it.
- An Original of a carol or a children's song may rate below Hard. It still comes last on the card.
- The guides of the Originals are written in English and German. Every other locale shows the English
  until its translation pass.
- The translation goals queue after this work, so they translate each new guide once.
- **Performance:** one more MIDI file per song, which the service worker caches with the rest.
- **Memory:** one more catalog row per song, and one more guide version per guide file.
- **Usability:** every song ends on the piece as it sounds. Many Originals are past a first-year
  learner, as the four catalog originals already are.
- **Simplicity:** the app keeps its five steps and adds no concept. The meter gains one rule of
  rating, and the song's model one optional version.

## Rejected

- **A fifth version under the same limits as Hard:** it would sound like Hard, and not like the piece.
- **Only reworking the four versions:** Beginner and Easy can never sound like the piece, so the
  learner would still never hear it.
- **Playing the source as a backing track under each version:** the versions move the key and the
  meter, so the two would not line up.
- **Rewriting the versions in place:** a learner's hand-set fingers would land on other notes, which
  breaks *Never lose or corrupt work*.
